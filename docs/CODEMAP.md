# Code map

Made by `node tools/codemap.ts` from the files' headers (#1023); never edited by hand. A row to each
file of `src/` (not `src/lib/`, game-engine, vendored), `tools/` and `types/`, sorted by path, and
its header's first paragraph; a folder where a box adds files is one row. A pull request that adds
a file, or changes what one owns, says so in the file's header and runs the tool: the `codemap`
suite names the rows of a stale map, and a conflict here is mended by running the tool. How the game
is put together is [SYSTEMS.md](SYSTEMS.md).

| File | What its header says first |
|---|---|
| `src/app/game.ts` | The Game: owns the world, the party, the rng and a stack of screens, and dispatches one input action per fixed step to the top screen. Screens are small objects with update/render, each in ui/ and written against the Host (ui/screen.ts), which the Game implements (#1022). |
| `src/app/input.ts` | Keyboard input as a queue of presses. Grid movement wants one step per press (with the browser's own key repeat for holding a key), so the game consumes discrete events rather than polling a held-state table. `held` is kept for the few places that want it (skipping a log). |
| `src/app/pilot/aims.ts` | The strategist (#720; docs/PILOT.md): what the pilot is for. The one quest's goal first, then a side or seeking quest's, nearest first; levelling where the goal's zone is above the company; walking the land where nothing else is known. It reads the world through Known alone: a goal's map once the journal shows the goal, a zone's band once the party has set foot in it. It says where it is bound, gives a goal up after three days with no way on, and turns back over a line into a zone too hard. |
| `src/app/pilot/explore.ts` | The explorer (#718; docs/PILOT.md): where the navigator has no seen way, it makes one. It walks to the nearest square with an unseen square beside it, searches the walls the seen map suggests a secret behind, and comes back to a way found shut once the company may open it. It reads the world through Known alone: a secret door is a wall to it until a search finds it, and a gate is learnt by trying it, never by its terms. Outdoors it keeps to one zone's box, as the world map draws the boxes. |
| `src/app/pilot/keep.ts` | The quartermaster (#719; docs/PILOT.md): keeps the company fit to fight, on screen. It rests where it stands by the harness's rule (`mustRest`), else goes where a need is met, the most urgent first: a temple for the dead and the stone, a camp or an inn for the tired, an inn or a shop for the hungry, a trainer for those ready and a shop for the best each can wear over the company's reserve, worn through the sheet. It reads the world through Known and a menu by its rows as they show (`ChoiceScreen.options`), and presses the keys a player would: a goal is the navigator's to walk (route.ts), a row is reached by `down` and taken by Space, and nothing is bought off screen. |
| `src/app/pilot/known.ts` | The pilot's fair view of the world (epic #713, Decided 1; docs/PILOT.md): what the party has seen and heard, and nothing more. Every brain reads the world through it and never through `g.world`, `g.maps` or a map as written: the automap's bits, its cells as the automap inks them, the features, exits and groups it marks, the maps walked, the world map as it is painted, the lines said and the company. Nothing here gives a map's secrets or encounters as written, a gate's terms or what a person or a statue holds, nor a shop's wares before its menu has shown them: a group is a square the automap marks, and no more. The headless run's omniscient view (#723), which the game never sets, has every square seen, each cell as it is and the secret doors as written (`secrets`). |
| `src/app/pilot/pilot.ts` | The pilot (epic #713; docs/PILOT.md): a mode that plays the game for watching. It lives on the Game, outside the save, built by src/main.ts or a test; it reads the world only through `Known` (known.ts) and presses the keys a player would, one action a paced tick, through `Game.update`. This is its frame: on and off, the tempo, the why line, the fights handed to the bot and the boxes closed. Which key it presses on the map is the brains' to say, each in a module of its own. |
| `src/app/pilot/purse.ts` | The pilot's purse (#719, #721; docs/PILOT.md): the reserve the quartermaster keeps back and the gold over it, which gear, a guild's fee and spells and a crossing may spend. A file of its own so that the navigator, which weighs a crossing's fare, reads the one rule the quartermaster and talk read. |
| `src/app/pilot/route.ts` | The navigator (#717; docs/PILOT.md): takes the party from where it stands to a square, over the squares it has seen and the exits it has seen, by the keys a player would press: `forward`, `turnLeft` and `turnRight`, never `back` or a strafe, so the viewer sees the party face where it goes. It reads the world through Known alone: a secret door is a wall to it until a search finds it, and a gate is learnt by trying it, never by its terms. |
| `src/app/pilot/talk.ts` | Talk (#721; docs/PILOT.md): everything the pilot says to anyone. It reads the world through Known and a menu or a riddle as the screen shows it, and presses the keys a player would: `down` to a row and Space, or Esc; a word's letters as the keyboard types them, then Enter. |

Once that is built it writes where the crossing puts a company down there (`landing`), and only then does the other end sell it: nothing is sold to a place that is not there to land in. | `src/game/inscriptions.ts` | Kiln-script (DESIGN §9; #434's call 2; #538), Act III's own mechanic.
