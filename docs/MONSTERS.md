# The Monsters of Caldera

*What the company fights down the road, area by area, and what each thing it fights tells it about
the world. DESIGN.md says what happens and STORY.md how it should feel; this is the creature half of
every area's brief (EXPANSION.md §8.2), to be carried into `docs/areas/<area>.md` as each area is
built. It is a draft, to take in or leave: where it and DESIGN.md disagree, the design wins. Figures
are measured on this branch at `5092f9c`.*

---

## 1. What the monsters are for

Three jobs. Where they pull apart, the earlier one wins.

1. **They are the gate.** Nothing on the road is locked (EXPANSION.md §2.2): what turns a company
   back is the monsters. An area's monsters let a company at its band's floor through and turn one
   two levels under it back (EXPANSION.md §5.2), and they do it by what they are and who they stand
   with, not by their numbers alone (DESIGN.md §6).
2. **They carry the secret.** The road starts among beasts and people and ends among machines, and
   nobody tells the company why. Each act shows a little more: the first thing that does not bleed,
   the first to carry the chisel's mark, a monk who walks like the keepers of the sleepers' bay.
   DESIGN.md §4 says that in the Underdeep the monsters stop being animals; that is the foot of a
   slope that starts on the Shelf.
3. **They are the land.** Each area's monsters come out of its ground, its weather and its hours:
   crows in the stubble, wreckers in the fog, pike under the ice, trolls that stand on the tors all
   day as stone.

The rules that follow from them:

- **Every monster is something.** Each is one of five kinds (§2), and its kind says what works on
  it. A company learns the rules on the Shelf and spends them all the way to the Core.
- **Meet it gentle first.** A family's first group on the road is its smallest and weakest, near
  the way in, so a company learns what the family does from a fight it can survive or run from.
  This is EXPANSION.md §5.2's warning, not a wall, for every family as well as every border.
- **Composition before numbers.** A hard fight is hard because of who stands with whom: the healer
  behind the soldiers, the leader the rest run without, the thicket in front of the oak. At most 12
  to a fight in at most three groups, and a bigger threat is a bigger monster (DESIGN.md §6).
- **Back changed, or not at all.** A family that returns brings new gear, anatomy or behaviour, as
  the wolf came back as the dire wolf and the rift hound; never the same drawing with bigger
  numbers. It is the anti-pillar against dead engine reuse, for monsters.
- **At most two or three new families an area** (EXPANSION.md §11), and variants on the frames that
  exist for the rest. §11 is the ledger.
- **The look says the role.** An archer carries a bow, a caster glows, a brute is big, a machine is
  smooth. The look is also the line the log says when a group is first seen, and every monster
  below has one, two lines of the log at most: it is how the danger is made legible (EXPANSION.md
  §7).
- **Every boss is a guardian.** It ends something or yields something, and never comes back
  (EXPANSION.md §5.1): the Hand of Ash yields the chisel, the Warden of the Cut the first Meridian
  journal, and each region's deepest boss can carry the next (DESIGN.md §10.3). It asks, too: won
  about half the time at the band's floor, nearly always two levels over (EXPANSION.md §5.2).
- **Found, never told.** No monster explains itself, and no fight's text names the machine. What a
  thing does and what it carries are the clue.
- **Plain names.** English words a listener could spell, as STORY.md is written for the ear, in
  British spelling. *Warden* is taken: the Charter, the Wardstones and the Rift's bosses (the Rift
  Warden, the Warden of the Cut) keep it, and nothing else gets it. No name says ship, hull or crew.

## 2. The five kinds

**Beasts.** The land's animals and plants, grown large in the Might and Magic way: rats, wolves,
spiders, the old wood, eels, bears, drakes. Sleep takes them, and they run when most of their group
is down. They carry no gold. Each has its own resistances rather than its kind's: the fire beetle
shrugs off fire because it lives in it. *Underneath:* they came in the ship, as the people did.
First on the Shelf; the last are the rats of the Underdeep, which nobody brought.

**People.** Brigands, smugglers, wreckers, the Compact's crews, the dwarves if the company crosses
them, the giants of the Stair, and the Ashen Hand. Sleep takes them; they carry gold and gear; most
break when their leader falls, and the Hand never does. *Underneath:* the crew's descendants, like
the company. First on the Shelf; the last is Vask.

**The dead.** Skeletons, barrow guards, drowned men, wights, the ash-cast dead of Old Cinder. Holy
light and Holy Strike bite; sleep and the druid's swarm do nothing, and they never run.
*Underneath:* nothing. They are the one kind the machine does not explain, and they stay the
fantasy's own. The Lanterns say the dead walk where the light is thin, and it is true: they gather
where a Stone is cut, and lie down when it is restored.

**The Rift.** Riftlings, rift hounds and crawlers, the elders, the wardens. Fast and hot, and their
touch paralyses. Cold bites the hot ones and fire the cold (§2.1); sleep takes the small ones and
never a warden; they stop coming when their Stone is restored. *Underneath (offered):* a Stone's
field, torn loose at the cut and set round whatever lies near it: ember and crystal at the Grove,
brine at the Tide Stone, slag at the Anvil. Its heart is a shard of the Stone, and a warden's heart
is the biggest, the humming lump STORY.md finds by the cellar's tear. First in the cellar; the last
are the lights over the bog round the dead ring on Cairnmoor.

**The machine.** The knockers of the Deep Mines, the tallymen, the keepers of the sleepers' bay, the
monks of the Whitespine, the stokers of Fire Mountain, and the things that live below. Lightning
bites. Sleep, the swarm and the Hearth's light do nothing: Smite and Wrath of the Hearth pass
through a machine, and the log says so the first time (*The light goes into it like a hand into a
glove.*), because the gods are the Custodian's voices and these are its hands. They never run, and
carry no gold, only parts. *Underneath:* the vessel's hands, each still doing the job it was given
four hundred years ago: inspecting, counting, tending, stoking, guarding. None of them is wicked;
the company is in the way. First at the bottom of the Deep Mines; the last at the Core.

## 3. What a monster can do

### 3.1 Today

A monster's whole turn is `monsterAct` in `src/game/combat.ts`. It picks one member, from the front
row unless it is `ranged` or the front row is down, rolls to hit against that member's armour, and
deals its dice. A hit may add a condition at a chance (`inflict`: poison, disease, sleep or
paralysis); ten monsters use it, and none for sleep. `ranged` lets it pick the back row, `missile`
marks the bows the weather spoils, and `mindless` shrugs off Slumber. It carries gold and drops.

That is all. No monster casts, heals, calls for help, runs, regenerates, resists an element or
stands behind another; monsters stand in no rows, so a weapon in the party's front row reaches any
of them. Nothing gives a curse or stone, though both conditions exist: Restore lifts a curse, and a
temple a stoning.

### 3.2 What the numbers show

**Holy Strike lands on the wrong things.** The paladin's +3 is keyed to `mindless` (`traitDamage`),
and its text says the mindless dead. Nine monsters are mindless: the five dead, and the Cellar
Slime, the Shore Crab and both of the Rift's wardens, which are not dead. A machine family built on
`mindless` would take it too. `kind` (§3.3) fixes it.

**One spell answers every fight past the floor.** The damage spells that scale do so with the
caster's level (their dice times half the level, rounded up), and the widest reach every foe. At
level 8 Meteor Swarm averages 46 to every foe (8d10, and 2 for Cassian's Spellfire), and every
regular monster in Thornmark but the ogre has 45 hit points or fewer. At level 10 it averages 57,
and the premade Cassian has about 73 spell points, five castings (averaged over 200 rolls). That is
the difficulty plain EXPANSION.md §2.2 measures: past the floor, every fight is won, and won the
same way.

Meteor Swarm gains about 5.5 a level; the line's hit points (§4.1) gain 3. Steepening every
monster's hit points to outrun it would leave the company's weapons behind, since they grow with
gear and a point of Might every other level, not with level. So the road past level 10 needs
monsters that one spell does not answer: kinds and elements that make the company choose (§2),
ranks that shield a back row, abilities that make the order of the kills matter, and more hit
points on the few (the elites, the brutes, the bosses) than a single casting takes. Whether a
spell's dice should stop growing somewhere is the systems lane's to weigh.

### 3.3 What the road asks

Each ask is a field on `MonsterDef` or `EncounterDef` and a few lines of `combat.ts` or `world.ts`,
made once in the systems lane and first spent by the area named, which waits for it (EXPANSION.md
§3, principle 2); no area invents one on the side. Each lands with the gate bot's answer to it
(EXPANSION.md §5.2), or the check will call a monster harder than it is.

| Ask | What it is | First needed | For |
|---|---|---|---|
| `level` | the monster's level, held to its map's band, give or take two | now | EXPANSION.md §5.2 |
| `kind` | beast, person, dead, Rift or machine, setting §2's defaults; a def may add its own (the slime and the crab keep their immunity to sleep) | now | Holy Strike on the dead only; the machines, by the Kilns at the latest |
| `look` | the line said the first time a group of it is seen, and the art's brief in one sentence | the Downs | the danger made legible (EXPANSION.md §7) |
| `when` | a group that walks only by night, in fog or snow, or in a season, read from the clock and the weather, so nothing new is saved | the Downs | the wreckers, the black dog |
| `until`, `after` | a group that stops coming back once a Stone is restored, or comes only after a step or a choice. Neither opens or shuts a way, so neither is a story lock (EXPANSION.md §2.3) | Thornmark | its Rifts closing with the Grove Stone; the old wood sleeping; the Anvil Guard; the Underdeep noticing |
| Ranks | a group's front and back: melee reaches the back once the front is down, and bows, slings and spells reach anyone | Saltreach, or Thornmark's retune if it wants it first | DESIGN.md §6's caster behind a wall of shields, which cannot be built today |
| Elements | fire, cold, lightning, nature and holy on the damage spells; `resist`, `immune` and `weak` on a monster | Saltreach, or the Deepthorn for fire on the wood | the first area past level 10, where one spell stops being every answer |
| Morale | people break when their leader falls and beasts when three in four are down, and leave the fight; the Hand, the dead, the Rift and the machine never do | Saltreach | the Barge Master and his crews |

| Drain | its hits heal it, or take spell points instead of hit points | Saltreach; spell points on Cairnmoor | the leeches; the bog lights |
| Regeneration | heals a set amount each round, except a round it took fire | Cairnmoor | the trolls |
| Curse and stone | `inflict` takes cursed and stoned, and stone gets a cure short of a temple | Cairnmoor for curse, the Wold for stone | the wights; the basilisk |
| Calls | a turn spent bringing a named group into the fight, within 12 and three groups | Rimewater | the tallymen; Vask |
| Sweep | one attack at every member of a row, with an element if it has one | the Whitespine; with fire, Ashfall | the giants; the drakes' breath |
| A light seen past the fog | a monster carrying a light is seen a square further than the weather allows (wanted, not needed) | the Downs | the lampmen |
| The Core's clock | the last fight counts rounds to the light going out | the Core | STORY.md, Act Five |

## 4. The numbers

### 4.1 The line

Give each monster the level of where it stands (the middle of its map's band, the gentlest a little
under), take out its role (§4.2), and the 28 regulars on the road today sit on one line. At level
*L* a soldier has about **3L + 4** hit points, armour **11 + L/2**, to-hit **1.5 + L/2**, and hits
for **3 + L/2** on average:

| Level | Hit points | Armour | To-hit | Damage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 10 | 12 | 2.5 | 4 |
| 8 | 28 | 15 | 5.5 | 7 |
| 16 | 52 | 19 | 9.5 | 11 |
| 24 | 76 | 23 | 13.5 | 15 |
| 32 | 100 | 27 | 17.5 | 19 |

A character's to-hit gains half a point a level (`attackBonus`), so armour on the line keeps the
chance of hitting a soldier the same at every level: Bram 75%, Wren 80% and Cassian 60%, at level 2
and at level 10 alike. A company's armour grows with its gear, not its level, so the line's to-hit
is what makes gear matter: in his starting gear, a soldier on the line hits Bram 38% of the time at
level 2 and 57% at level 10. The gear the curve gives a company at each level (EXPANSION.md §5.2)
is what holds that.

The line is where an area starts, not where it ends: the gate check tunes it, and what it tunes is
a cost. What a company at the floor has over one two levels under it is mostly hit points and spell
points: the premade six have about 155 and 90 between them at level 5, and 105 and 55 at level 3
(averaged over 200 rolls), with spell tier 3 landing between, at level 4. An area holds its gate
when the fights of its road, from one rest to the next, cost more than the smaller pool and less
than the larger. A group's cost is the damage it deals in the rounds it lasts, so its damage and
its staying power are two halves of one number, and `tools/gate.ts` sets the number.

The road adds three roles on a soldier's numbers: the **leader**, whose fall breaks its group; the
**healer**, which mends its own; and the **caller**, which brings another group.

### 4.3 Pay, and size

**Pay.** Experience and gold are the curve's (EXPANSION.md §5.2): a clear pays for its band whatever
the area's size, so what a monster pays comes from its area first and its role second. Inside an
area pay follows risk. Thornmark's, for scale: the dire wolf and the thorn spider 120–130, soldiers
and archers 140–190, the adept and the rift hound 240–250, the bone knight and the wraith 280–330,
the elder 440, the ogre 520, and the bosses 2,000 and 3,000. Beasts carry no gold, and machines
carry parts, which is one place the Ember Stone's can come from (DESIGN.md §9).

**Size.** Today it runs from 0.35, the rat, to 1.25, the ogre. A lone monster stands 34 + 92 × size
pixels (`combatHeight`) in a view 268 high: the ogre 149, a size of 2 about 218, four-fifths of the
view, and 2.5 fills it. A pair stands as tall as one. That is room for the bigger monsters of
DESIGN.md §6: the heartwood, the giants, the drakes, the crawlers below.

## 5. Act I: Ashes on the Road (levels 1–10)

*Why are the Stones failing?* Every kind but the machine is met here, each at its gentlest, and the
act ends on a Stone cut with tools from below.

### 5.1 The Shelf (band 1–5; built)

| Kind | On the Shelf, in the cellar and at Greywater |
|---|---|
| Beasts | Giant Rat, Cellar Slime, Wolf, Wild Boar, Marsh Spider, Shore Crab |
| People | Bandit, Bandit Archer, Smuggler, Smuggler Bowman, the Smuggler Captain; and the Hand's first ranks, the Ashen Cultist, the Ashen Acolyte and the Ashen Deacon |
| The dead | Skeleton, Drowned Man, Ghoul |
| The Rift | Riftling, Rift Crawler, the Rift Warden |

It stays as built. What this asks of it:

- `kind` on every def: the slime and the crab are beasts, the Rift Warden is the Rift, and Holy
  Strike stops landing on them.
- The Rift Warden leaves its heart behind: the humming shard STORY.md finds by the tear, the first
  thing on the road with the Hearth's hum in it, heard again under the Hearth.
- The rats are the first thing most companies kill. The road brings them back twice, aboard the
  Tide Ship and at the bottom of the world (§9.2).

### 5.2 Harrow Downs (band 2–5; the pilot)

*The chalk hills west of the Shelf, the fields round Gullwick, and the coast past Harrow Light to
the Ledge. The Shelf's weather: mild, wet, and foggy off the sea in the autumn.*

The Downs are the night the light went out, seen from the shore. The fishermen steer by the Hearth;
with it failing the coast is dark, and the dark has people in it who were waiting for it (STORY.md,
Act One). Up on the hills the Queen's barrow stands open, and her guard is up. The Downs are the
pilot's zone map (EXPANSION.md §9, Phase 1), so their monsters test the pipeline and not the
resolver: one new family, variants on the frames that exist, and two small fields.

**New:** birds, the frame the road reuses for owls, herons, gulls, ravens, eagles and vultures.
**Back:** the Wolf on the hills, bandits and their archers on the coast road, the Shore Crab under
Harrow Light.

**Asks:** `look` and `when`. Flyers are `ranged` without `missile`, which exists.

### 5.3 Thornmark (band 5–10; built, retuned in the pilot)

*The old forest over the pass: Thornhold, the old tower, the barrow, the river, and the Grove.*

Thornmark is where the Stone is cut, and every kind the Shelf showed is here at full strength:
packs of dire wolves, spiders that poison, brigands with archers, the Hand in numbers, the barrow's
knights and wraiths, and the Rift the cut let through. The roster stays. What the pilot changes is
the gate: today a company wins nine fights in ten here at level 3 (EXPANSION.md §2.2), and the gate
wants one in four at most. The monsters' levers, in order:

- **The gentlest first.** The first groups past the pass are wolves and brigands, and the crossing
  line says how the land feels (EXPANSION.md §5.2).
- **Groups that ask.** The adepts behind the zealots, once there are ranks; the brigands' archers
  with the ogre; thorn spiders poisoning the front row before the dire wolves come in.
- **The cost, between the pools** (§4.1), set with `tools/gate.ts`.

When the Grove Stone is restored, Thornmark's Rifts close (DESIGN.md §9): its rift hounds and
riftling elders stop coming back, and so do the barrow's dead (`until`).

### 5.4 The Deepthorn (band 8–10)

*The oldest of the forest, south of Thornmark down to Thorn Head, and the oldest elf-hold, which
keeps the treaty. Thornmark's winter: cold, and snow that lies for weeks.*

The Deepthorn is age: trees older than Harrow in a wood older than the elves' memory, and a treaty
sealed with the chisel's mark, the first time the machine's script is seen on something that is not
a tool. Its monsters are the wood. The old wood woke when the Grove Stone was cut, and it does not
know friends from cutters; when the Stone is restored it sleeps again (`until`), and the elves can
walk their own paths.

**New:** the old wood. **Back:** the Dire Wolf in bigger packs, the Thorn Spider, the Ogre alone in
the deep; the Great Owl from the birds.

- **The oak behind the thicket**: a heartwood over three brambles, the brute behind a wall that

**Asks:** `until` and `when`; elements, if the wood is to burn before Saltreach.

## 6. Act II: The Salt Road (levels 10–16)

*Who profits?* The act follows the cargo down the Long Water, out to the smugglers' ship and back
to the Sunder, and ends on Vask's name and a wall no one built. Its monsters are the first past
today's level cap, and the first that one spell must not answer.

### 6.1 Saltreach (band 10–12)

*The Long Water's country: the Upper Water's willows and Reedholm's fields, the Delta's fen and the
Drowned Temples, the Saltings' pans and Saltmouth, the free port. Salt flats and tidal ground.*

Saltreach has lost its Stone. The Tide Stone went downriver on a barge at midsummer, so its Rifts
are open and the fen glitters with brine glass; the temples are dark, and their drowned priests
count instead of singing; the Compact's barges carry shards and people down the Long Water. Most of
the road's new abilities are first spent here, which adds to what Saltreach already waits on in
Phase 2 (EXPANSION.md §7, §9).

**New:** long bodies (the eels first; later the worms and the pike) and toads. **Back:** the
Drowned Man in the temples; the Rift, in brine (§2.1); the spider frame as the salt crab; the Grey
Heron from the birds.

## 12. What this adds to the design

To take in or leave, as STORY.md's were:

- Five kinds, with `kind` on every monster in place of `mindless`'s two jobs, so that Holy Strike
  lands on the dead and nothing else.
- The machine's rules: each is doing a job; lightning bites; the Hearth's light passes through it,
  because the gods are the Custodian's voices and these are its hands.
- The Rift as a Stone's field torn loose and dressed in the Stone's material, with a shard for a
  heart; the Sunder staying open when its warden falls; no Rifts in the Whitespine, whose Stone is
  whole, or in Ashfall, whose Stone never made a field, so that on the far side the machine is near
  the surface instead.
- The dead gathering where a Stone is cut and lying down when it is restored, and the old wood
  waking and sleeping with the Grove Stone.
- No machine before the bottom of the Deep Mines and no Rift after Cairnmoor, held by a check.
- The keepers of the sleepers' bay putting people to sleep, and the monks as keepers under their
  robes, so that a company knows the walk.
- The Hand's hands greying with rank, as the Hand of Ash's are already drawn (`cultist.ts`), from
  the overseers' wrists to the Grey Hands' shoulders.
- The rats, the first thing killed and the last animal met.
- The moths, drawn to light from Lanternwood to the Hearth.
- The drowned chanters' count and the monastery's bells, both ending on the eleven.
- The giants' toll on the Stair.
- Vask's signet commanding the core's machines, and the Core's fight on a clock.

Where this and DESIGN.md disagree, the design wins.

### Open questions

1. **The Compact's crews.** The bargemen and the Wrack smugglers work for the Compact, and the
   Compact is the Thief's promotion path (DESIGN.md §8). Either killing them costs Standing, or the
   crews who carry the Hand's cargo have left the Compact for the Hand's coin. The Compact's line
   should say which.
2. **What the giants are.** They are not among the sleepers, and every people came in the ship
   (DESIGN.md §7). Either they came awake, or they are something else. The monsters need only the
   surface; the design may want the rest.
3. **The reach's bands.** The atlas gives the Buried Tower 26–28 and the Ice Caves 20–22, where the
   reach is at the cap (DESIGN.md §9). The Ice Caves' way in lies in band-20 country, which is
   right; their monsters are the cap's.
4. **What a fight is worth.** From Saltreach on, an area spans two levels over as much as fifteen
   zone maps of land, where Thornmark spans five levels over fewer than six. At the slice's density
   of groups a clear would pay several bands, unless each fight pays far less of a level than
   Thornmark's do.
   The curve settles it (EXPANSION.md §2.1, §5.2), but it decides whether a fight on the road is
   worth having, and the country's floor of groups may want to sit looser than its floor of
   features (EXPANSION.md §5.3).
5. **Spells without a ceiling.** §3.2's gap is the systems lane's as much as the monsters': a
   ceiling on a spell's dice would do some of what resistances do, more bluntly.
